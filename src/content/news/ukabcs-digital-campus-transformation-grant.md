### 1. Call at a Glance
Applications must be submitted only through the official route. No work or expenditure may begin before executed documents and written start authorisation.

---

### 2. Why This Grant Exists
Digital transformation is no longer achieved by uploading lecture files or purchasing a software licence. When courses, assignments, examinations, attendance and communications are spread across disconnected tools, students lack a clear learning journey, academic staff repeat administrative work and leaders have limited visibility of quality and delivery. 

This grant helps a faculty establish one governed digital-learning ecosystem. It combines institutional design, LMS build and configuration, complete digital preparation of academic content, visual and video production, assessment and course automation, hosting and security, staff development, student onboarding, responsible AI, pilot delivery, launch, handover and sustainability. 

The intended result is not merely a website. The faculty receives an operational platform, configured courses, approved digital content, trained teams, operating documentation and a realistic plan for continued service.

---

### 3. What Is an LMS?
A Learning Management System (LMS) is the digital environment that brings teaching and learning administration together. It can organise faculties, departments, programmes, terms and courses; manage users and permissions; publish lessons, files and videos; collect assignments; run quizzes and examinations; manage attendance and announcements; provide feedback; and generate authorised reports. 

- **Students** see their enrolled courses, deadlines, activities and permitted results.
- **Academic staff** manage teaching and assessment from one centralised location.
- **Administrators and quality teams** monitor approved information and compliance workflows.

A well-designed LMS reflects the university's academic rules and governance rather than forcing the institution into a generic template.

---

### 4. Why This Matters for Universities
Universities face changing student expectations, wider use of blended learning, rapid AI development, demand for professional and continuing education, and stronger expectations around data, accessibility and service continuity. A governed LMS enables the university to respond consistently, deliver approved online and blended activities, develop summer and professional programmes, improve access to content and use evidence for improvement. 

The programme treats digital learning as future academic infrastructure, not a temporary technical project. Institutional ownership, staff capability, secure data and supplier exit are central.

---

### 5. Programme Objectives
- Build and configure an LMS for each participating faculty.
- Prepare approved academic material as complete, usable digital courses.
- Produce agreed visual designs, recordings, edited videos and course introductions.
- Automate assignments, quizzes, examinations, attendance and communications where authorised.
- Support approved training, workshops, conferences, summer courses and payment-enabled activities.
- Train administrators, academics, quality teams, technical teams and students.
- Integrate Ebonics as an optional voice tutor and AI assistant.
- Provide hosting, security, backup and time-limited support.
- Transfer documentation and operational ownership to the university.

---

### 6. Route One: Industrial Companies
An industrial company may apply to become an award-holding allocation and coordination partner. A successful company may nominate up to five universities. It must identify a qualified implementing company, or present an acceptable competitive method for appointing one, and demonstrate that it can maintain separate records and quality controls across its portfolio. 

The industrial company receives **£2,000 per approved faculty** from the UKABCS share for evidenced university engagement, coordination and reporting. It cannot make the final grant decision, promise funding before UKABCS approval, collect the UKABCS contribution, or impose undisclosed services or fees.

---

### 7. Route Two: Direct University Applications
An authorised university may apply directly for one or more faculties. It appoints a Principal Investigator (PI) for each faculty. UKABCS appoints or provides a qualified implementing company. The PI receives **£2,000 per faculty** from the UKABCS share, subject to approved milestones and legal, tax and conflict-of-interest arrangements.

---

### 8. Who May Apply?
- **Industrial applicants**: Legally registered companies able to contract, invoice and coordinate university delivery in Egypt.
- **University applicants**: Officially recognised public, national, private and non-profit higher-education institutions in Egypt, subject to final legal eligibility rules. A faculty must have formal university authority.
- *Individuals, students and individual academic staff may not apply independently.*

---

### 9. Minimum Industrial Company Requirements
Lawful status and authority, relevant institutional or technology experience, an ethical university-engagement plan, a qualified implementing company or procurement route, portfolio capacity, conflict and anti-bribery controls, secure records, transparent financial understanding and willingness to accept monitoring and audit.

---

### 10. Minimum University Requirements
Senior sponsorship, an authorised project owner, one PI per faculty, academic and technical leadership, quality and data/legal contacts, a finance or procurement contact, approved academic content and rights, staff time, participation in filming and training, institutional co-funding of £12,000 per faculty, and a post-grant operating plan.

---

### 11. What the Faculty Receives

#### 11.1 Readiness and Design
Examination of academic structures, current processes, content, systems, users, infrastructure, data and risks. Produces a baseline, target operating model, roles, course architecture, quality approach and implementation plan.

#### 11.2 LMS Build and Configuration
Faculty and department structures, programmes, terms, courses, user roles, enrolment, course templates, assignments, quizzes, examinations, attendance, announcements, feedback and reporting. Workflows are comprehensively tested before launch.

#### 11.3 Digital Academic Content Production
Structuring approved academic material into lessons and activities, formatting and uploading resources, connecting assessments, adding metadata, checking presentation across devices, and delivering complete configured courses for faculty approval.

#### 11.4 Filming and Visual Production
Course visual identity, presentations, illustrations, infographics, filming, audio and video editing, captions, lecturer introductions and course trailers.

#### 11.5 Hosting, Security and Backup
Hosting, servers, basic licences, secure connections, monitoring, role-based access, backup, recovery planning and security updates.

#### 11.6 Ebonics: Optional Voice Tutor and AI Assistant
Ebonics is designed to stay close to the student during study. A student may speak or type to request an explanation, simplify a difficult idea, review permitted course material, ask follow-up questions, analyse an educational image or organise a study session. Within approved permissions, it may use course, book or user-provided document context and respond by text or voice. 

- Academic staff may use Ebonics to support explanations or draft practice questions that remain subject to their review.
- There is **no subscription charge** for the LMS or ordinary enrolled courses. Ebonics is entirely optional at £2 per month for each student user during the first six months. After that period, a discounted university-student price will be based on LLM usage. Non-subscription never restricts ordinary course access.

#### 11.7 Smart Reading Tools
Approved tools such as **GetDocsTalk** help students and researchers ask page-linked questions about PDFs, save notes and tasks, listen to explanations and work through research papers.

#### 11.8 Training, Pilot and Handover
Role-based competence training demonstrated through practical tasks. Controlled pilot tests with real users and workflows. Full administration rights, documentation, knowledge transfer, and sustainability handover.

---

### 12. Academic and Administrative Automation
Automation of assignment opening and closing, submission confirmation, reminders, quiz attempts, authorised examination rules, enrolment, attendance, completion and reporting. The university may also configure summer courses, professional programmes, workshops and conferences with automated registration and approved payment gateways.

### 14. Payment Transparency
UKABCS pays its share directly to implementing companies, suppliers and approved coordination recipients. The university pays its share directly to the implementing company under contract and invoice. The university pays nothing to UKABCS. No unrestricted cash grant is transferred to the university or industrial company. Payments require evidence, milestones and acceptance.

---

### 15. What Is Not Automatically Included
Unlimited content or videos, full academic authorship, physical devices, building works, perpetual hosting or support, unlimited AI usage, unapproved integrations, accreditation, guaranteed enrolment, income, or external academic outcomes.

---

### 16. Student Charging Principles
Ordinary platform and course access carries no separate student subscription. Ebonics is optional at the published price. Paid summer or professional activities are separate, university-authorised services. Charges must be transparent, and optional AI non-subscription may not restrict an ordinary course.

---

### 17. Application Process & Assessment
Applicants select the correct route (Industrial Company or Direct University), confirm authority, answer application questions, attach the required files and submit through the official channel. Applications undergo completeness and eligibility screening, independent merit assessment, moderation, and due diligence.

---

### 18. Required Documents

#### Required Industrial Company Documents (3 Attachments)
1. Governance, coordination, reporting and quality plan.
2. Data, security, conflicts and procurement plan.
3. Relevant experience and verified evidence file.

#### Required University Documents (5 Attachments)
1. Institutional support letter.
2. Faculty/faculties and PI details.
3. Need, baseline and current-systems description.
4. Initial course/content and rights inventory.
5. £12,000-per-faculty commitment and procurement plan.

---

### 19. Important Conditions & UKRI Disclaimer
Application and eligibility do not guarantee an award. Each faculty is a separate £30,000 funding unit. One faculty is the minimum; three is a preference. Only executed award documents create final obligations.
